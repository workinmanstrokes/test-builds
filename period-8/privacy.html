Privacy Policy: Period 8

Developer: Diddlerrr
Contact: mscofield1588@gmail.com
App: Period 8 (Android package com.diddlerrr.period8)
Effective date: November 7, 2026

This policy explains what information the Period 8 Android app ("the app", "we") handles, why, and the choices you have. The same game can also be opened in a web browser. Period 8 is a gore-free school mystery/horror game for players aged 13 and older.

Summary

  • No account, no sign-up, no friends list, no chat, and no cloud save of your game. We never ask for your name, email address, phone number, precise location, contacts, photos or files.
  • Your progress stays on your device.
  • Screenshots you make in the game stay on your device unless you choose to share them.
  • Optional rewarded video ads are provided by Google AdMob. There are no forced or pop-up ads.
  • Optional gameplay statistics are off until you turn them on in Settings > Share anonymous gameplay stats. Saves from older versions, including ones that stored that switch as on by default, stay off until you turn it on yourself. On Android, statistics also stay off unless Google's consent result allows them where that consent applies.
  • When those checks pass, the app can send data to two Google services: Firebase Analytics, and one Firestore session summary per visit in Google's Firebase project diddlerrr-analytics. Turning the switch off stops new collection and discards statistics that have not been sent yet.
  • The app does not offer in-app purchases and does not use Google Play Billing.
  • We do not sell your personal information.

1. Information stored only on your device

Your progress (current run, endings found, anomaly journal, best scores, settings such as sound, vibration, jump-scare intensity and whether you opted in to statistics, and counters that limit how often optional ads are offered) is saved in the app's local storage on your device. We do not receive that saved game and cannot view or restore it.

Settings > Reset progress deletes that saved game on this device. It also deletes the on-device analytics install id and any statistics that have not been sent yet. It does not delete statistics already received by Google. You can also clear the app's storage or uninstall. If Android backup is turned on, Android may include this on-device data in your own Google account backup. That backup is controlled by you and Google, not by us, and restoring it can bring the saved game back.

There is no online account, social graph, leaderboard, or multiplayer session.

2. Screenshots and clip results

The in-game screenshot button and the clip-mode "Save result card" button create an image on your device. The app saves it temporarily in its own cache and opens Android's share sheet so you can save or send it wherever you choose (for example your gallery or a messaging app). We do not receive these images. Anything you share is then handled by the app or service you share it with.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The Android app offers optional rewarded video ads that you choose to watch in exchange for a hint or for keeping your streak after a mistake. There are no forced or pop-up ads, and no interstitials.

Ads are provided by Google AdMob. When ads are requested or shown, Google may collect:

  • device identifiers, including the Android Advertising ID;
  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate your approximate location (country/region);
  • ad interaction data (for example, whether an ad was shown, watched or tapped);
  • device and app information and diagnostic data (device model, OS version, app version, performance data).

Google uses this information to deliver, measure and limit the frequency of ads, to personalise ads where permitted, and to prevent fraud and abuse. Where required by law (for example in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, or under US state privacy laws), the app asks for your consent through Google's consent tool before the ad SDK is started and before an ad is requested. If consent is required and not obtained, or if the consent check fails, ads are not requested. You can change your choice from Settings > Privacy options when that button is shown. You can also reset or delete your Advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in your Android device settings.

The Advertising ID permission remains in the app because AdMob uses it for rewarded ads. The ads library also merges the Android ad-services permissions for the advertising id, attribution, and topics. Those are part of how Google may measure and select ads. The same library removes a boot-completed permission, and that permission is not in the release manifest. The statistics switch does not turn these ad permissions off. Choosing not to share gameplay statistics does not by itself stop AdMob from using the Advertising ID when you ask to watch a rewarded ad.

A web copy of the game does not use AdMob. It may show a short local stand-in instead of a video. If you play a copy embedded on Poki or CrazyGames, that site's SDK may show the rewarded video and process data under that site's own policy. The Android app does not load those site SDKs.

Learn more: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

4. Optional gameplay statistics

Statistics are optional and off by default. Nothing in this section is sent until both of these are true:

  1. You turn on Settings > Share anonymous gameplay stats. A saved game from an older version, where that switch was on without a separate opt-in, is not permission. You have to turn it on in this version.
  2. On the Android app, Google's consent result allows it. That means the result is "not required", or consent was obtained and Google reports that ads may be requested. If the result is missing, still required, or the consent check fails (for example while offline), statistics stay off. A web copy has no Google consent form; only the settings switch applies there.

Turning the switch off, or a later Android consent result that no longer allows collection, stops collection immediately. Statistics still only in memory are discarded and are not uploaded. The on-device install id is removed when you are not opted in, when you turn the switch off, and when you reset progress. If you leave the switch on but Android consent later refuses collection, an install id already stored from a visit that was sent can remain on the device until you turn the switch off or reset. An upload that has already been handed to the network cannot be pulled back.

4.1 Firebase Analytics

The app can use Firebase Analytics to understand, in aggregate, how the game is played, for example: the app being opened, return visits by day, loops started, whether each loop was answered correctly and which anomaly it was, hints used, which ending was reached, use of clip mode and the screenshot tool, and whether optional ads were offered or completed. Along with these events, Firebase collects an app-instance identifier, the Advertising ID where available, device information (model, OS version, language), app version, and approximate location (country) derived from your IP address.

The Android app ships with Firebase collection disabled, and with analytics and ad storage denied until the app enables them after the checks above. Google's measurement library also merges permission to bind to the Play install-referrer service, so when collection is on it can learn which store listing led to the install. We do not use these events to identify you by name. They do not include screenshots or anything you type.

This uses Firebase project period-8-dd on the free Spark plan. Learn more: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

4.2 Firestore session summary

When the checks above pass, the app can also write one summary row per visit to Cloud Firestore in Google's Firebase project diddlerrr-analytics (free Spark plan). The row is built only after you opt in. It is uploaded when the visit ends (for example when you leave the page or the app). If you opt out before that upload, the row is dropped and is not sent.

The row contains: a random install id and a random session id created on the device; the game name and version; whether the visit looks like Android or web; the date; how many visits and how many days since the first opted-in visit on that device; session length; counts of loops started, completed and failed; the highest loop index; a miss count; time spent in loops; rewarded-ad counts by a short placement name (hint or keep); and counts for a "remove ads" screen that this version does not show. It does not include your name, email, phone number, the words in the game, or screenshots. The install id is not created until you opt in.

Google receives the connection, so Google can see the IP address of the device that uploads the row even though the row itself does not contain the IP address.

We do not run Cloud Functions or any other paid backend for this.

5. In-app purchases

This version has no in-app purchases. The "Remove ads" control is not shown. The app does not include the Google Play Billing library and does not request the billing permission. No payment is taken and no purchase identifier is sent. If a real "Remove ads" product is offered later, this policy will be updated first. The code still contains a disabled scaffold for that product; it does not contact Google Play.

6. Permissions

The Android app and the Google libraries it includes use:

  • internet access, and a network-state check, to load rewarded ads and, only after the checks in section 4, to send statistics;
  • the Advertising ID (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID), kept because AdMob rewarded ads use it, plus the ad-services advertising-id, attribution, and topics permissions from the ads library;
  • permission for Google's measurement library to bind to the Play install-referrer service;
  • vibration, for haptic feedback, which you can switch off in Settings;
  • a wake lock declared by the Firebase Analytics library;
  • a foreground-service permission declared by AndroidX WorkManager, which is pulled in with those Google libraries. Period 8 does not start a foreground service of its own and does not show an ongoing notification for one;
  • an internal signature permission created by AndroidX so its own components can communicate. That is not a request for your data.

The release manifest does not include the Play billing permission, location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, or boot-completed. Gameplay does not require a network connection. Ads and optional statistics do.

7. Sharing and selling

We do not sell personal information and we do not share it for money. When you use the features above, information is collected by and shared with Google (AdMob, Firebase Analytics, and Firestore) as described. A web copy embedded on Poki or CrazyGames is also subject to that site's policy. We may disclose information if required by law.

8. Data retention and deletion

Progress stays on your device until you delete it with Settings > Reset progress, by clearing the app's storage, or by uninstalling, subject to Android backup as described above.

Unsent statistics are discarded when you turn the switch off, when Android consent no longer allows collection, or when you reset progress. They are not uploaded.

Firestore session rows that have already been received stay in the diddlerrr-analytics project until they are deleted by hand. The free Spark plan does not expire those rows, and this app has no login and no server of its own that can delete one person's rows. They are stored under a random id, not your name or email, so a request often cannot be matched to a document.

Firebase Analytics hits are kept according to the Google Analytics property. Where that property is linked, the retention period is 2 months (Google Analytics' default). The app cannot delete an individual hit. AdMob data is retained by Google according to Google's policies.

There is no account to delete. The app does not offer an account-deletion control, because that control would not reach statistics already stored by Google and would overstate what it deletes.

9. Security

Data sent to Google is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). We do not operate our own servers, and there is no paid backend. Gameplay progress is not uploaded. Optional statistics, only after the checks in section 4, are stored by Google.

10. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 used the app and statistics were sent, email us. We will delete Firestore rows we can actually match and tell you what we cannot match. We cannot promise that every AdMob or Firebase Analytics record has been erased, because those records are held by Google and are not tied to a name.

11. Your choices and rights

  • Ads: change ad consent in Settings > Privacy options (where shown), or reset or delete your Advertising ID in Android settings. Refusing consent stops ad requests. It does not delete ads Google has already served.
  • Statistics: the switch starts off. Turn on Settings > Share anonymous gameplay stats only if you want collection. Turn it off to stop collection and drop unsent statistics. On Android, Settings > Privacy options (where shown) can also withdraw the Google consent that statistics require.
  • On-device game data: Settings > Reset progress, clear the app's storage, or uninstall. This removes the saved game and the on-device analytics id. It does not remove statistics already stored by Google.
  • Scares: switch jump scares to Gentle in Settings.
  • Requests: depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR, UK GDPR or US state laws such as the CCPA/CPRA) you may have rights to access, delete or object to processing of information about you. Email mscofield1588@gmail.com. We will reply within 30 days and say what we could delete. Because there is no account, the practical path on the free Spark plan is manual. We can delete a Firestore session document in the Firebase console when we can identify it. We often cannot, because the row has no name or email. Firebase Analytics and AdMob records can only be handled with Google's own tools, and without an app-instance id or Advertising ID we may be unable to locate them. The app does not display those identifiers. We will not describe a deletion as complete when we could not reach the copy Google holds.
